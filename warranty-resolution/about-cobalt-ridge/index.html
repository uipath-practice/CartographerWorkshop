    About Cobalt Ridge Automation¶

    Cobalt Ridge Automation builds industrial automation and material-handling equipment, in other words: the machines that keep goods moving inside warehouses and factories (conveyor belts, sorters and the controls that run them). You probably never notice this kind of equipment, until it stops. When a customer's sortation line goes down, getting it running again involves people across the company, and no single process ties their work together.

    What's a sortation line?¶

    Picture a big parcel hub on a busy morning. Thousands of packages arrive on conveyor belts. Scanners read every barcode, and the system pushes each package down the right chute, toward the right truck or the right order. That automated sorting system is a sortation line, and Cobalt Ridge sells them to its customers.

    When a sortation line goes down, the warehouse can't ship. Packages pile up, trucks leave half empty and orders go out late. Cobalt Ridge calls this a P1 line outage, the most urgent kind of problem there is. Every hour it lasts costs the customer money and patience.

    What's a warranty claim?¶

    When a customer buys a line, Cobalt Ridge promises to fix it for free if something breaks during an agreed period. That promise is the warranty. A warranty claim is the customer calling to say: "It broke, it's still covered, please fix it."

    Cobalt Ridge made the equipment and made the promise, so the claim lands on its desk. Every claim costs it parts, technician time and sometimes credits to an unhappy customer. And there are a lot of them:

    ~7,400warranty claims a year
    11.4 daysmean time from customer report to restored line
    96 hoursmean downtime for a P1 line outage
    $23.8Mannual warranty spend

    Seven teams, one relay race¶

    Getting a broken line running again isn't one person's job. Seven functions pass the claim along like a baton:

    SupportWarranty administrationEngineeringPartsField serviceFinanceQuality
    1. Support takes the customer's call and logs the problem.
    2. Warranty administration checks whether the equipment is still covered. Today that decision waits for a weekly review, so a claim can wait up to a week for an answer.
    3. Engineering works out what's actually wrong.
    4. Parts finds and ships the replacement. Sometimes it swaps in a substitute part and nobody writes it down.
    5. Field service sends a technician to fix the line on site.
    6. Finance records what the repair cost, often late.
    7. Quality should spot the parts that keep failing. Often it never hears about them.

    The baton doesn't travel well. The work is spread across six systems and one very long email thread:

    Support Cloud (CRSC)WarrantyTrack WT-9AssetVaultHelios CRMSAP S/4HANAFieldLink FSMEmail

    People retype the same details from one system into the next, handoffs get lost in inboxes, and repeat failures go unnoticed because nobody connects the dots. Add it all up and you get 11.4 days per claim and $23.8M a year.

    Your mission¶

    Cobalt Ridge's leadership has set a clear target for FY2027. You'll use Cartographer to redesign the process so it gets there.

    The brief for FY2027

    11.4 days→3.5 daysmean resolution time
    96 hours→24 hoursP1 line downtime

    People keep authority over coverage, safety, engineering, substitution, restoration and finance decisions. Your job is to design how.
